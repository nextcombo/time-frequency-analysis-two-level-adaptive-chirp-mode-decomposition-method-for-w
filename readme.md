# [Time-Frequency Analysis]Two-Level Adaptive Chirp Mode Decomposition Method for Wheel Flatness Detection under Variable Speed Conditions [Attached MATLAB Code]

Introduction: The health condition of railway wheels directly affects the stability and safety of vehicles [1]. During abnormal braking processes or adverse environmental conditions (such as rain, snow, etc.), this reduces the wheel-rail adhesion [2]. Wheel slipping can lead to severe wear or abrasion on the wheel tread surface (also known as "flat spots" on the wheel). As one of the most common local defects in railway wheels, wheel flat spots generate significant impact forces and noise, affecting passenger comfort, leading to performance degradation of both vehicles and infrastructure components, and even causing derailment accidents [3]. In recent years, with the increase in vehicle axle weight and speed, the dangers associated with wheel flat spots have become increasingly prominent. Therefore, the status monitoring and fault detection of wheel flat spots are becoming increasingly important in modern rail transit industries.
Over the past few decades, various wheel flatness detection methods have been developed based on different measurement technologies and types of measurement data. For example, many researchers have installed strain sensors (such as strain gauges or fiber Bragg gratings [3,4]) on railway tracks to detect and identify wheel-rail impact caused by wheel flatness. Additionally, Bayesian prediction [5] and machine learning [6] methods can be applied to strain data to improve fault detection accuracy. Ultrasonic technology has also been reported to be used for detecting and quantifying wheel flatness [7]. In addition to the aforementioned methods, we are particularly interested in onboard wheel flatness detection methods based on vehicle component vibration signals. Vibration signal-based fault detection methods have gained widespread attention due to their sensitivity to early faults and ease of measurement.Bernal et al. [8] studied the wheel flatness detection capability based on vibration acceleration signals, finding that under noise conditions, vibration signals from wheel pairs (or axleboxes) and steering frame can be used for fault detection. Bosso et al. [9] developed an onboard fault diagnosis algorithm for wheel flatness based on time-domain statistical indicators of axlebox vibration acceleration. However, railway vehicles often experience complex wheel-rail excitations (e.g., while the fault signal characteristics of wheel flatness (especially early faults) may be overshadowed by strong interference components, making fault detection very challenging.
To detect wheel dents under interference conditions, advanced filtering and signal decomposition methods can be employed to enhance and extract fault signals. Li et al. [10] proposed an adaptive multi-scale wavelet filter for enhancing the characteristic of pulse signals caused by wheel dents in strong noise. They also utilized Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) to extract components of the faulty pulse signals and calculated the envelope spectrum of these components to detect the fault characteristics frequency of the wheel dents [11]. However, EMD is known to have inherent limitations such as mode mixing, end effects, and poor noise robustness, which prevent it from correctly extracting the required fault signals under complex excitation conditions.Numerous improved versions of EMD have been developed, such as integrated EMD [12] and the recent time-varying filtering-based EMD (TVF-EMD) [13], but these methods only address specific issues. As an alternative to EMD, more advanced methods have been constructed, such as empirical wavelet transforms [14] and variational mode decomposition (VMD) [15]. Some of these methods have shown potential in fault diagnosis for railway vehicle components [16-19]. It is worth noting that sparse representation is also a well-known method for signal decomposition in fault diagnosis. For example, Qin has constructed a new model-based pulsed wavelet family and proposed a sparse representation algorithm for fault feature extraction using this wavelet dictionary [20].He proposed a new one-dimensional fast K-SVD algorithm with an adaptive transient dictionary for transient feature extraction [21]. However, most of these methods are developed based on narrowband conditions and may suffer from over-decomposition issues in broadband signals (e.g., instantaneous impulses caused by wheel ruts). Additionally, envelope spectra cannot characterize the time-varying characteristic frequencies of wheel ruts during vehicle deceleration or acceleration processes (such as traction or braking).
Time-frequency analysis is an effective tool for extracting characteristics of time-varying signals. Various types of TF techniques, such as wavelet-based methods, Short-time Fourier Transform (STFT), and Wigner-Ville distribution, have been used for analysis or detection of wheel flats [22-25]. However, the Time-Frequency Representation (TFR) generated by these traditional methods is known to have drawbacks such as poor energy concentration and cross terms, and therefore cannot accurately reveal the TFR patterns of time-varying signals [26]. Yang et al. [27] applied a post-processing technique called TF redistribution to improve the readability of the TFR of vehicle vibration responses caused by wheel flats under varying speed conditions. This paper focuses solely on the analysis of vibration characteristics without considering fault detection under interference conditions.As a special case of redistribution technology, Synchronous Squeezing Transient (SST) has attracted increasing interest due to its similar pattern retrieval capability to EMD [28, 29]. Many extensions of SST, such as Multi-SST [30], Matched SST [31] and Higher Order SST [32], have been developed and successfully applied in mechanical fault diagnosis [33]. However, as known, methods based on SST are not very effective at handling strong time-varying signals and cannot correctly distinguish closely spaced signal patterns. Recently, the author has constructed several powerful Time Frequency (TF) methods based on Adaptive Linear Frequency Modulation Pattern Decomposition (ACMD), which can generate high-resolution TF for rapidly varying signals with very close or overlapping patterns [34-36].Although the ACMD method has been applied in multiple fields [37-40], its application in wheel flatness detection has not yet been reported. Considering the complex interference of track irregularities, the adaptability and specificity of ACMD in wheel flatness detection need to be improved.
In summary, wheel flatness detection under variable speed conditions is a challenging task that has received little attention in the literature. The difficulties include: 1) strong interference that may mask fault signal characteristics; 2) time-varying feature frequencies, making accurate identification difficult. Based on the vehicle-track coupled dynamics model, this study analyzes the vibration characteristics of vehicles under variable speed conditions, particularly the frequency evolution patterns caused by wheel flatness. To address the above two challenges in wheel flatness detection, a targeted two-stage ACMD method based on wheel vibration characteristics is proposed. The contributions of this method include: 1) combining ACMD with a pattern recombination scheme based on the Gini index (GI) to separate different signal patterns (e.g., fault impulses, harmonics, etc.); 2) applying an TF method based on ACMD to the separated signal patterns, accurately extracting time-varying feature frequencies, thereby detecting wheel flatness and other rail damage (Level 2).
